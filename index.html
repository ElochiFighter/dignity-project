Periods Should Never Cost Someone Their Dignity.

Dignity Cycle is a community-centered initiative working to address period poverty by increasing access to menstrual products, education, and resources for people experiencing financial hardship and housing insecurity.

A period is a normal part of life. Having access to the products needed to manage one should be normal, too.

Our Mission

Our Mission

Our mission is to increase access to menstrual products, education, and resources while reducing the stigma and mental-health burden associated with period poverty. Through community partnerships, fundraising, donation drives, and education, Dignity Cycle works to promote dignity, equity, and opportunity throughout the Atlanta community.

We believe that menstrual care is more than a health necessity. It is connected to education, confidence, mental well-being, and the ability to participate fully in everyday life.

72 Days of Dignity

72 days. One community. A commitment to dignity.

72 Days of Dignity is our focused campaign to turn awareness into action.

Over 72 days, Dignity Cycle is working to raise funds, collect menstrual products, educate our community, and connect resources with people who need them.

Our goal is simple: make menstrual care more accessible while reminding people that they deserve dignity at every stage of their lives.

Throughout the campaign, we will share our progress, highlight ways the community can participate, and work toward creating an impact that continues beyond these 72 days.

What We're Working Toward

Increasing access to menstrual products

Supporting people experiencing housing insecurity

Raising awareness about period poverty

Reducing stigma surrounding menstruation

Connecting menstrual health with mental well-being

Building a community that values dignity and equity

Our Impact

This project started with a simple question:

What can I do to help?

Since beginning Dignity Cycle, we have raised funds and collected menstrual products to support people in our community. Our work has included fundraising, community outreach, educational efforts, and donations of menstrual supplies.

But the goal isn't just a number.

Every product donated represents one less person having to choose between menstrual care and another basic need. Every conversation helps challenge stigma. Every person who gets involved helps make the issue harder to ignore.

Why It Matters

Period poverty can affect far more than someone's ability to access menstrual products. When people cannot reliably access the supplies they need, it can create barriers to school, work, social activities, and everyday life.

Dignity Cycle focuses on the human side of the issue: access, opportunity, mental well-being, and dignity.

No one should have to feel ashamed because they cannot afford a basic necessity.
